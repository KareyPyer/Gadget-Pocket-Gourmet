# 🥢 Wafu Dressing / Sauce (和風ドレッシング / 和風ソース)

> *"A diplomatic treaty between Eastern umami and Western salad culture, emulsified in a single drop of sesame oil." — Dr. Umami*

---

## 🧬 Identity

- **Japanese Name:** 和風ドレッシング / 和風ソース
- **Romanization:** Wafu doresshingu / Wafu sōsu
- **Category:** Sauce / Dressing
- **Origin:** Japan (Modern fusion, inspired by traditional vinaigrettes and dashi)
- **Flavor Profile:** Umami-rich, bright acidity, savory, slightly sweet, aromatic

---

## 🧪 Description

Wafu sauce (or Wafu dressing) is a Japanese-style vinaigrette that beautifully bridges the gap between Western salad preparations and traditional Japanese seasoning. 

It typically combines the deep umami of soy sauce and dashi with the bright acidity of rice vinegar, rounded out by the nutty, toasted aroma of sesame oil.

Dr. Umami classifies it as:

> "A biochemical handshake between the ocean (dashi) and the earth (sesame), designed to elevate raw vegetation to gourmet status."

---

## 🍜 Traditional Uses

- Fresh green salads (especially with crispy lettuce, tomatoes, and onions)
- Cold tofu (Hiyayakko)
- Lightly blanched vegetables (Ohitashi)
- Carpaccio of white fish or beef
- Cold noodle salads (Soba or Udon)

---

## 🧬 Core Ingredients

- Shoyu (soy sauce)
- Rice vinegar (Komezu)
- Sesame oil (Goma abura)
- Dashi (kelp or bonito stock, sometimes powdered)
- Sugar or mirin (for balance)
- Grated onion or ginger (optional, for aromatic depth)

## 🧊 Storage

- Store in a sealed container in the refrigerator.
- Shake vigorously before each use (natural separation of oil and vinegar is a sign of authenticity, not spoilage).
- Best consumed within 1–2 weeks if homemade.
